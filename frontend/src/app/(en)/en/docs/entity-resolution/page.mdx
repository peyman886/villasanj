## The problem and the cost of errors

The two kinds of error do not cost the same. A **wrong merge** sends the user to a different villa
and destroys the trust the product is built on; a **missed merge** only loses some value. So the M5
bar is end-to-end precision ≥ 95% with a Wilson lower bound ≥ 92%, and among the policies that clear
that bar we pick the one with the highest recall.

The research report had not foreseen the hardest case: **complexes that use one promotional photo
for several units.** Photo-based matching sees these units as one, although each is a separate
rental unit. This case later led to a revision of the gold-set labels as well (below).

## The pipeline

<Diagram
  name="er-pipeline"
  caption="blocking → evidence → score → judge in a zone → human queue → constrained clustering"
  locale="en"
/>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Blocking (recall first)">
    The union of several sources: normalised location with room count ±1, pHash buckets (any shared
    near-identical photo), k nearest DINOv2 neighbours at photo level, and overlapping distance
    ranges. The goal: find ≥ 98% of true pairs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evidence">
    Photo-set similarity (best one-to-one match, with pHash and image cosine) with **frequency
    weighting**: a photo that appears in several listings carries almost no weight. Alongside it,
    differences in rooms, capacity and floor area, the distance range, the nightly price ratio and
    the similarity of rare phrases in the text.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A transparent rule score">
    A weighted sum of the evidence with each part's contribution, so every merge can be explained on
    the page. The gold set sets the threshold. We did not use Splink: our strong evidence is
    pair-level, and the missed pairs are not separated by reweighting the same evidence.
    <Adr n="0014" locale="en" />
  </Step>
  <Step title="An LLM judge in one score zone">
    For pairs scoring −2 to 3, the judge sees a photo grid of both listings and the structured
    facts, and returns "match", "non-match" or "unsure" with its confidence.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Human labels and clustering">
    The owner's label is a must-link or a cannot-link, and no machine decision overrides it.
    Clustering is greedy from the strongest pair, and each villa gets at most one listing per
    platform.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The gold set: the owner's labels

We claim no precision without human labels, and an LLM label is never the ground truth of an
evaluation. gold-v1 is a stratified queue: geographic score bands, photo score bands, same-platform
pairs and the wide net, ordered so that the sample stays balanced wherever labelling stops. Each
stratum's weight in the evaluation comes from its population, so precision and recall are reported
**weighted**, and the Wilson interval uses the effective sample size.

<Diagram
  name="labeling-loop"
  caption="The labelling loop: queue, blind tool, label, evaluation and villa rebuild"
  locale="en"
/>

- **Blind tool:** `/label` does not show the system's score or why the pair was picked, so the
  labeller's judgement is not anchored. The keys are M, N and U, and they also work on a Persian
  keyboard.
- **Protocol:** `docs/er-labeling-protocol.md`. The question for each pair: "Are these two listings
  the same real rental unit?". Rule 2: different units of one complex are `N`, and if only shared
  spaces are visible, `U`.
- **Volume:** 362 pairs in about 7.4 hours, 73 seconds per pair on average.

## The rule threshold

The ADR-0009 rule: the lowest score whose precision is ≥ 95% and whose lower bound is ≥ 92%. The
curve below shows the weighted precision and recall of the rule score at each threshold, on the
revised labels; the marker is the threshold the gold set picks for "rules alone".

<ErCurve locale="en" />

After the label revision, "rules alone" has to raise its threshold so far to reach the precision bar
that half of the recall is lost. That is the main reason the judge was brought in as a guard of
precision.

## The LLM judge

For each pair the judge receives two photo grids (composite grids, because Gemini bills about 1,090
tokens per image regardless of size) and the structured facts, and returns a structured verdict with
confidence and reason. The prompt is versioned, and the reason for every verdict is stored.

### Model comparison

Three models, each on its own, on the same gold pairs in the −3 to 3 zone. The first run (3 Oct)
gave cost and latency: gemini-3.8-flash cost about $0.32 for 142 pairs, with a p95 of about 34
seconds. After the label revision the same responses were re-scored from the cache, without a single
call:

## The decision policy

<Diagram
  name="er-decision-policy"
  caption="The current policy: outside the zone the rules decide; inside it the judge confirms or vetoes rule merges, and a new merge is up to a human"
  locale="en"
/>

<ConfigSnippet file="er.toml" locale="en" />

Four approaches that need to be kept apart:

| Approach                                       | Who merges                                                                           | Who vetoes                                    | Status                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rules alone                                    | score ≥ threshold                                                                    | nobody                                        | does not clear the precision bar at −0.25 on the revised labels                                    |
| Full judge                                     | the rules, and the judge below the threshold                                         | the judge                                     | excellent point precision, but the lower bound is below 92%: too few labels in the geographic band |
| Advisory judge (policy of 3 Oct)               | the rules                                                                            | nobody; the judge only orders the human queue | after the label revision it has the same precision as "rules alone" and fails                      |
| **The judge vetoes, a human merges** (current) | the rules, unless the judge disagrees or is unsure inside the zone; and human labels | the judge (a confident "non-match")           | clears the precision bar with the same recall as the rules                                         |

**Merges a human reviewed** are counted separately: every "match" label from the owner is a
must-link, so the judge's suggestions (pairs below the threshold that the judge is confident match)
are merged only when a human confirms them. Recall rises only with precision a human has confirmed.

How the current policy behaves inside the zone: a confident "match" on a pair above the threshold is
the rule merge itself; below the threshold it goes to the human queue as a "suggestion". A confident
"non-match" vetoes the rule merge. "Unsure", or any verdict with confidence below 0.8, holds the
merge back, and the pair waits in the human queue.

<ErPolicies locale="en" />

<Callout kind="caution" title="An optimism to be aware of">
  The judge was chosen on these same gold pairs, so the end-to-end numbers of the policies that use
  it are optimistic by that much. The clean test is a gold-v2 sample of new villas. The judge's
  merges below the threshold also stay off until labels show their precision in the geographic band:
  that stratum has about 30 labelled pairs, each standing for about 84 candidates.
</Callout>

## What changed after the human review

### History of the label revision

1. **2 and 3 Oct, the M3 results:** 41 of 45 same-platform pairs were labelled "match", although
   their titles named different units ("unit 4" and "unit 7", three bedrooms against two, up to 946
   metres apart). They were outside the cross-platform evaluation and were left alone; the owner was
   told.
2. **3 Oct, the end-to-end evaluation:** the judge vetoed five cross-platform pairs labelled "match",
   and in all five it named the numbers of different units of one complex. Because arbitration
   between the judge and the labels belonged to the owner, the judge was made "advisory" only.
   <Adr n="0014" locale="en">
     amendment of 3 Oct
   </Adr>
3. **4 Oct, the owner confirms:** the owner confirmed that these pairs had been labelled "match" by
   mistake. 48 labels were revised under rule 2, each with its reason: 41 same-platform (38 to
   "non-match" and 3 to "unsure") and 7 cross-platform (4 to "non-match" and 3 to "unsure"). The
   owner's own "unsure" labels were left alone.
4. **The same day, every measurement rerun:** the matching evaluation, the threshold curve, the
   judge bake-off (from the cache), the villa rebuild, H1 to H3, H4 and the ROADMAP tables.

The revisions did not erase history: `er.label_revision` keeps the original label, the new label,
the reason, the reviser and the time, and `er revise-labels` applies all of them or none: if a
pair's current label does not match the file, no revision is applied, and running it again changes
nothing. The evaluation always runs on both versions of the labels so the effect of the revision is
visible.

### Effect on the metrics

<ErWhatChanged locale="en" />

In short: the new wrong merges of "rules alone" are exactly the complex units, and the judge vetoes
all of them without losing a single correct gold pair. Under the ADR-0009 rule (the highest recall
that clears the bar), `judge_vetoes` was switched on and `judge_merges` stayed off. The number of
villas went up slightly, because pairs that had been merged by mistake are now apart.

## Clustering and stable ids

<Diagram
  name="villa-reconciliation"
  caption="Merges, splits and id reconciliation between two villa builds"
  locale="en"
/>

- **At most one listing per platform** in each villa: product rule 4, both in the domain aggregate
  and through the unique index on `(villa_id, platform)`. A merge that would break it is refused and
  reported, never forced.
- **Merge order:** human labels first, then the strongest score. A human cannot-link blocks every
  machine merge.
- **Stable ids:** each new build is reconciled with the previous one by the largest member overlap;
  merges and splits are recorded in `er.villa_event`. A bug that left one id on two villas after a
  split was found and closed with a test.
- **Reproducibility:** rebuilding with the same labels gives the same villas and the same ids (M5
  criterion 7).

## The human queue

Waiting pairs are added to the `er-human` queue (append-only: a rerun adds nothing, and labelled
pairs stay as history). The order: the judge's suggestions first, then its disputes of rule merges,
then the unsure ones; within each group, the most confident first. Every label rebuilds the villas.

The owner labelled all 376 pairs in the queue, choosing "unsure" or "another villa" where there
were no photos, and sometimes checked the listing on the platform's own site to make sure two
listings were not different units of one property. Because these are pairs the judge itself picked,
they measure the judge inside its own zone, not matching precision on a random sample:

<ErHumanQueue locale="en" />

Every "match" from the owner is a must-link, so these labels increased the number of villas on both
platforms (the cards at the top of the page). 97% of the judge's suggestions were confirmed, but the
interval's lower bound is still below 92%, so the judge's automatic merges stay off for new pairs.
The judge's vetoes were confirmed again by the same labels.

## H5: photos against the other evidence

Hypothesis H5 said that photos alone are not enough for precision. The ablation measures each part
of the evidence on its own:

Photos alone do not reach the precision bar, because units of one complex share photos; the other
evidence alone does not reach it either; together they do. H5 is confirmed.

## Reproducing

```bash
make match                                   # photo hashes and embeddings, blocking, evidence, scores
cd backend
uv run villasanj er queue --name gold-v1     # the stratified queue
uv run villasanj er revise-labels ../eval/labels/gold-v1-revisions-2026-10-04.toml
uv run villasanj er report                   # reports/er-eval-<date>.md and .json
LLM__PROVIDER=offline uv run villasanj er judge-eval --out ../reports/judge-eval-<model>-<date>.md   # bake-off from the cache
uv run villasanj er villas                   # villas with the config/er.toml policy
uv run villasanj er villas-eval              # end to end on gold with the labels not applied
```
