## Query understanding

<Steps>
  <Step title="LLM with structured output">
    The query becomes a `SearchIntent` under a strict schema: places, group size, rooms, dates as a
    relative expression ("next weekend"), budget and its basis, amenities, driving time, and wishes
    the schema has no field for. The current route is gpt-5.4-mini, with gemini-3.1-flash-lite as
    the fallback.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Number guard">
    Every number the model returns must appear in the query text; places and out-of-schema wishes
    must also appear verbatim in the query. If they do not, the model is asked once more with the
    list of violations, and then the field is dropped. As a result, no invented numeric condition
    reaches the search.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Budget basis">
    "Per night" or "in total" is kept only when the query says so in words (A19). Otherwise the
    basis is unknown; the search shows the result count under both readings and asks the user.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Dates by code, not by the model">
    Relative expressions are resolved with the Solar Hijri calendar and holidays: Fridays, fixed
    solar holidays from `config/holidays.toml` with their sources, and lunar holidays only when
    Jabama's calendar has flagged them. Beyond the horizon that has been observed, the result says
    "lunar holidays unknown" instead of guessing (A12).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Removable chips">
    The system's reading is shown as chips, and every chip can be removed; removing one never adds a
    number, and the search reruns from the same cached understanding. Wishes that cannot be checked
    ("sauna", "large yard") come back with "we cannot check these wishes".
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Ranking

The full rules and the constants the code actually uses are on [How we rank](/how-we-rank); that page
reads its numbers from `GET /search/ranking`.

1. **Exclude only on a stated fact:** a night that was not free at the latest observation, capacity
   below the group size, fewer rooms than asked for, a minimum price above the budget, an amenity
   the listing itself says it lacks, and a driving time above the cap even in the best case. The
   reason for every exclusion is counted and shown.
2. **Unknown does not exclude:** unpublished capacity, an unknown fee or an unconfirmed amenity
   stays in with a warning; a distance claim the map contradicts is also a warning, not an
   exclusion.
3. **Order:** listings whose requested amenities are confirmed come first, then the rest. Within
   each group, the score is the sum of two named parts: price per person per night (60%, relative
   to this search) and the guest rating (40%, with a Bayesian prior so that two five-star votes do
   not weigh as much as a hundred). Each part's share is visible on every card.
4. **No commission:** there is no advertising or commission factor in the rank.

### Grouping by villa

A villa that is on both platforms is one result, ranked by its best listing. The card also shows the
other platform's offer with its own source (or that it was not free there) and links to the villa
page. Prices are never merged.

### Driving time and "near the sea"

The driving-time cap is compared with the best case of the uncertainty range; a listing whose range
crosses the cap gets a "may be longer" warning. A coverage note says how many results remain with a
cap of 3, 4, 5 and 6 hours. "Near the sea" means within 1 km in a straight line from the coast
(A17): the map confirms it when the whole blur circle is within that distance, and contradicts it
when no part of the circle is.

## The "why this villa?" explanation

<Steps>
  <Step title="Code builds the facts">
    Price, rating, review count, distance, driving time, availability with the age of the
    observation, and the comparison with the next result; each one a fact with an ID and a source.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The model only writes sentences">
    The model puts a slot such as `{F1}` wherever a number goes. A deterministic renderer fills the
    slots with Persian numerals.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verifier">
    The text is rejected if it contains a raw digit, uses an unknown slot, states a comparison that
    contradicts the facts, or presents availability as a fact ("is available"). Fewer than two facts
    is not accepted either.
  </Step>
  <Step title="One retry, then a template">
    Rejected text goes back once with its violations; if it is rejected again, a fixed template
    states the same facts. The same template is used when no model answers.
  </Step>
</Steps>

| --------------------------- | ---------- | -------- | ------- | ----- | ------ | ----------- |
| **gemini-3.8-flash** (kept) | 100%       | 0%       | 4       | 6.4 s | 10.6 s | $0.014      |
| gemini-3.5-flash-lite       | 100%       | 0%       | 7       | 1.5 s | 3.0 s  | $0.006      |
| gemini-3.1-flash-lite       | 100%       | 0%       | 11      | 2.6 s | 3.3 s  | $0.006      |
| gpt-5.4-mini                | 90%        | 10%      | 5       | 3.1 s | 4.9 s  | $0.033      |
| gpt-4.1-mini                | 80%        | 20%      | 14      | 3.5 s | 4.3 s  | $0.013      |

Every text shown has passed the verifier, so the choice was about prose: the lite models meet the
latency target but place the slots in broken sentences. Decision: keep gemini-3.8-flash and move the
explanation off the critical path of the results. The search page fetches the results without an
explanation first, and the explanation is streamed afterwards.

## Latency

<PerformanceTable locale="en" />

A search with cached understanding and no explanation takes about two seconds; no criterion set a
target for it. The 4 October profile (cProfile, one cached search): about 0.6 s for the
holiday-flag query, which aggregates the whole calendar observation table every time (561 thousand
rows after the same-window capture); about 0.7 s loading all listings (twice); about 0.7 s building
each listing's offer for the query dates; and about 0.3 s for the location verdicts. Every search
ranks the whole catalogue in memory, and the calendar table grows with every capture; the 2 October measurement, before the same-window capture, was 1.1 s. A separate table summarising
the holiday flags and loading the listings once are the ways to make it faster.

## Retrieval and ranking evaluation

Retrieval is filtering plus transparent ranking. Full-text (FTS) and vector (pgvector) search were
not built, because M8 criterion 2 says vector retrieval ships only if it improves nDCG@10 by at
least 0.03, and that cannot be measured without human grades.

For those grades, 30 real queries with set dates and group sizes (`eval/relevance/queries-v1.txt`)
were run once, and three orders of the same filtered villas were stored: the current ranking,
cheapest first and best rated first. The top 20 villas of the current ranking and the top 10 of each
baseline were pooled (946 villas) and shown to the owner without rank or system name, in random
order: relevant (2), partly relevant (1), not relevant (0). The owner fully graded 11 queries and
closed the criterion on that basis:

<RelevanceResults locale="en" />

The current ranking beats both baselines on nDCG@10: on 9 of 11 queries against "cheapest first" and
on 10 of 11 against "best rated first". The owner said grading was hard and almost every villa could
be called relevant; 88% of the grades are 1 or 2, so these numbers show an overall advantage, not
fine differences. Full-text and vector search were not built, so there is nothing to compare them
with.
